# Strategy, value, and risk appetite

Enterprise AI strategy is a portfolio of business changes, not a model-adoption target. It should state where AI is expected to create value, where it must not be used, what shared capabilities the enterprise will fund, and how leaders will know when to scale, change, or stop.

## Executive decisions

Leadership should make these decisions explicitly:

- which outcomes and strategic capabilities AI may accelerate;
- which affected stakeholders and harms receive heightened protection;
- the enterprise risk appetite by impact, autonomy, exposure, and data class;
- which capabilities are centrally governed or operated;
- how product teams receive funding and remain accountable for outcomes;
- which vendors, deployment models, and concentration levels are acceptable;
- which evidence reaches executive, board, and audit oversight;
- when regulatory or sector-specific requirements override general guidance.

## Portfolio thesis

Group opportunities by value mechanism:

| Value mechanism | Example measures |
|---|---|
| Revenue and growth | conversion, retention, time to market, new capability adoption |
| Productivity | cycle time, handling time, throughput, rework |
| Quality and service | accuracy, resolution, consistency, satisfaction |
| Risk reduction | loss avoided, earlier detection, control coverage |
| Knowledge leverage | search success, onboarding time, expert capacity |
| Strategic learning | capability maturity, reusable assets, validated options |

The business case should include the full workflow. Time saved by generating a draft is not realized value if review, correction, escalation, or new failure handling consumes the gain.

## Prioritization

Score proposed use cases across:

- measurable outcome and accountable owner;
- quality and availability of authoritative data;
- process readiness and ability to redesign the workflow;
- feasibility with current model capability;
- consequence and reversibility of error;
- reuse of existing platform capabilities;
- delivery and operating cost;
- stakeholder impact and adoption readiness;
- regulatory, supplier, and concentration constraints;
- learning value for the wider portfolio.

Prefer bounded use cases with measurable outcomes over broad “enterprise chatbot” mandates.

## Risk appetite

Risk appetite should produce architectural consequences. Examples:

- external communication requires provenance and human review until defined quality thresholds are sustained;
- restricted data may use only approved regions, providers, endpoints, and retention settings;
- irreversible actions always require transaction-bound approval;
- autonomous operation is limited to reversible actions with bounded loss, complete traceability, and tested circuit breakers;
- high-impact decisions require independent validation, meaningful recourse, and periodic reassessment;
- no production use may depend on personal accounts or credentials.

Record prohibited practices separately from uses that are temporarily unsupported.

## Funding model

Fund three layers:

1. **Shared foundations** — identity, data, network, security, model access, evidence, and observability.
2. **Platform product** — paved roads, templates, registries, evaluation, and support.
3. **Use-case products** — workflow change, domain data, user experience, evaluation, operations, and benefits realization.

Central funding can accelerate shared capabilities, but domain funding and ownership keep value accountability close to the process.

## Benefits realization

For each use case, maintain:

- baseline and counterfactual;
- benefit hypothesis and measurement window;
- adoption and workflow-change assumptions;
- quality, harm, cost, and stop metrics;
- attributable versus correlated outcomes;
- total cost to build, assure, operate, support, and exit;
- named owner for corrective action or retirement.

Use controlled experiments where ethical and practical. Where causal measurement is difficult, state the uncertainty rather than reporting model activity as business value.

## Executive dashboard

Report a balanced portfolio view:

- value delivered and confidence in attribution;
- use cases by lifecycle, domain, owner, and risk tier;
- quality, fairness, safety, security, and recourse signals;
- material incidents, complaints, exceptions, and overdue actions;
- platform adoption, delivery lead time, and duplicated solutions;
- spend, forecast, unit economics, and concentration;
- supplier changes and model deprecations;
- skills, literacy, and operating-capacity gaps;
- systems scaled, paused, or retired and why.

Do not reduce the dashboard to pilot count, token volume, or estimated hours saved.

## Workforce and change

Plan for:

- role and task redesign, not only tool training;
- AI literacy proportional to decision rights;
- manager and reviewer capacity;
- employee consultation and lawful monitoring;
- accessibility and accommodations;
- updated procedures, performance measures, and segregation of duties;
- support for correction, escalation, and reporting;
- shadow-AI discovery paired with usable sanctioned alternatives.

Meaningful adoption requires users to understand limitations and retain authority to challenge outcomes.

## Scale, hold, or stop

Scale when the use case demonstrates outcome, acceptable residual risk, stable operations, sustainable economics, and organizational readiness.

Hold when evidence is incomplete, controls are not operating, a supplier or regulatory dependency is unresolved, or the workflow cannot support meaningful oversight.

Stop when value does not justify total cost and risk, critical thresholds remain unmet, the use case loses its legitimate purpose, or a safer alternative is superior.
