# Adoption roadmap

The roadmap builds a repeatable delivery system before scaling the portfolio.

## Phase 1 — Establish control and vocabulary

Outcomes:

- executive sponsor and accountable cross-functional owners;
- shared definitions, use-case intake, inventory, and risk tiers;
- initial approved environments, models, and data rules;
- two or three bounded use cases with measurable outcomes;
- baseline incident and exception processes.

Exit signal: the enterprise can identify what AI systems exist, who owns them, and which controls apply.

## Phase 2 — Create paved roads

Outcomes:

- managed model access and routing;
- reusable identity, secrets, policy, retrieval, evaluation, and observability;
- standard production-readiness evidence;
- automated versioning and staged release;
- central patterns with domain-level ownership.

Exit signal: a product team can ship a Tier 1 or Tier 2 use case without inventing baseline controls.

## Phase 3 — Scale with evidence

Outcomes:

- portfolio dashboards for outcome, risk, quality, cost, and incidents;
- reusable domain evaluation sets;
- mature third-party and concentration management;
- independent assurance for consequential systems;
- workforce enablement and community of practice;
- capacity and unit-economic planning.

Exit signal: growth in use cases does not create proportional review bottlenecks or hidden risk.

## Phase 4 — Optimize and adapt

Outcomes:

- automated control evidence and continuous reassessment;
- model and vendor portability exercises;
- systematic retirement of low-value systems;
- incident learning incorporated into patterns and tests;
- regulation and policy change integrated into the control library.

Exit signal: the architecture can absorb model, vendor, demand, and policy changes without losing control.

## First 90 days

1. Appoint the business, technology, security, data, privacy, and risk owners.
2. Inventory existing and planned AI systems.
3. Approve a common classification and intake form.
4. Select a small portfolio spanning low and moderate risk.
5. Establish an approved model-access path and isolated experimentation environment.
6. Define evaluation and production-readiness evidence.
7. Instrument outcome, quality, reliability, security, and cost.
8. Run a failure exercise and document the first reusable pattern.

Do not lead with a large platform procurement. Let validated use cases and control requirements shape the platform backlog.
